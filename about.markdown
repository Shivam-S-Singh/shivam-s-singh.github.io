# Hello There! I'm Shivam Singh
I'm a Network Engineer and System Administator with a passion for building, maintaining, and troubleshooting reliable IT infrastructure. I enjoy the challenge of diagnosing complex network issues and finding practical solutions that keep systems running smoothly.

My interests lie at the intersection of networking and developer operations. As I continue to grow in my career, I'm focused on strengthening my NetDevOps skills by combining traditional networking knowledge with modern automation and infrastructure practices.

## Professional History ##

I first got my feet wet in technology by building my own high-end PC for ***“productivity”***, guided by countless YouTube tutorials. Around the same time, I joined my high school’s Technology Squad, where I gained hands-on experience with fundamental IT and system administration tasks. My responsibilities included deploying disk images using Norton Ghost, performing hardware upgrades, and practicing proper cable management.

After high school, I decided to pursue a Bachelor of Science in Information Technology, specializing in Network Communication. My degree is currently in progress, with my final project underway. During my studies, I had the opportunity to oversee the computer lab at my school, which gave me valuable access to network equipment and allowed me to further develop my practical networking skills through hands-on training.

### Service Desk at DirecTV ###
My IT career started as a Service Desk Coordinator and quickly became the main onsite point of contact for day-to-day IT operations. At the time, the original IT team included a Network Administrator, Application Analyst, Service Desk Administrator, and System Administrator. As the business scaled back due to performance challenges, the onsite team was reduced, while regional teams continued to handle specialized systems and networking responsibilities.

That meant I had the opportunity to take on a wider range of responsibilities and wear several different hats. I wouldn’t describe myself as a one-person IT department, because I wasn’t—the regional teams were still there to provide support where needed. But being the only operational IT staff member onsite required me to be adaptable, take ownership, and work closely with both employees and remote IT teams to keep things running smoothly.

That experience taught me how to manage competing priorities, communicate with people at different technical levels, and stay resourceful in a changing environment. It also gave me a strong foundation in IT operations and shaped the way I approach problem-solving and support today.
